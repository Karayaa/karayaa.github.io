Liebherr Web & App
Designsystem 2.0

A unified, token-driven design system powering Liebherr's web platforms and native apps across multiple product divisions.

Role

UI Designer

Timeline

12 months · 2025-26

Tools

Figma, Tokens Studio, Specs

Status

Live · in production

Overview

Replacing three legacy systems with one shared foundation.

Liebherr's digital landscape spans dozens of product sites, configurators, and customer-facing apps across 13 divisions. The previous seperate systems had lots of similar components but no unified approach.

Designsystem 2.0 is a complete rebuild: a token-first architecture, a shared component layer for both web and native, and contribution guidelines that let internal product teams ship faster without breaking visual consistency.

  • Three-tier token system (primitive → semantic → components)
  • 60+ components covering web portal and native apps
  • Themeable across two major brands (Corporate and Home Appliances)
  • Accessibility compliance (WCAG 2.2 AA)
  • Structured documentation and specification guidelines
The Challenge

How do you unify 13 divisions without flattening them?

The brief was deceptively simple: one design system to rule them all. The reality was politics, technical debt, and multiple teams each protecting their own conventions.

The hardest part wasn't drawing components — it was negotiating naming, governance, and a contribution model that felt fair. We ran weekly sync sessions with division leads an dpower users throughout the whole process, plus a public Figma library that anyone could comment on.

Constraints we worked with: Legacy CMS that couldn't be replaced, accessibility floor of WCAG 2.2 AA, and shipping in production within one year.

The Approach

A token-first system, themed per division.

We split the system into three clear layers — primitives (raw values), semantics (intent), and components (assembled). The components were completely rebuilt from the ground up to utilize the token system and to unify the design language.

Documentation lives directly in Figma so designers can access it alongside the design files. We treated docs as a product: they got the same review process as components.

  • Migrated 2 legacy systems into the unified library
  • Unified the design language across all products
  • Published design guidelines
Outcome

Powering multiple products inside Liebherr.

Ten months in, Designsystem 2.0 is live with the Corporate Website being the first product using the new design system.The bigger win is cultural: design and engineering finally share a vocabulary, and new product teams can ship a feature-complete site in weeks instead of quarters.

The system continues to evolve through a public ux community approach — the goal was never to finish it, but to bring it alive.

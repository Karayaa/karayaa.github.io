Overview

Carrying a 77-year-old identity into its next decade.

Liebherr is a family-owned manufacturer founded in 1949, with a brand that's been refined steadily over decades. The 2020 evolution wasn't a rebrand — it was a tuning. A distinct typeface, a recalibrated color system, refined photography direction, and a digital-native motion language.

My role was to assist in translating the new identity into rollout assets across the group's digital surfaces: web, social, product UI, and internal templates.

  • Updated brand guidelines for analog anddigital application
  • Assets for multiple design software
  • Motion principles for video & UI
The Challenge

Evolution, not revolution — a much harder design problem.

Employees had to feel the upgrade. Existing materials needed to age out gracefully over multiple months — no big bang, no asset bonfire. That's the brief that makes evolution rollouts genuinely tricky: every decision has to honor what was there.

We built a tiering system — Tier 1 assets (homepage, hero campaigns) updated immediately; Tier 2 (product pages, brochures) over twelve months; Tier 3 (internal docs, signage) at natural replacement cycles. This kept the rollout fundable and avoided wasting good materials.

The Approach

A tiered rollout with a shared asset library.

The deliverable wasn't a guideline PDF — it was operational. We built a brand center where every division could pull assets, request additions, and see live examples of correct application. Designers and marketers across regions pulled from the same source, with regional flexibility baked in.

  • Brand Center with 800+ on-brand assets
  • Quarterly governance review with division leads
  • Migration scorecard tracking adoption per division
  • Reduced asset request time from weeks to hours

Outcome

One brand, applied with discipline.

Within six months, Tier 1 assets were fully migrated, and internal asset requests dropped by 70% as teams self-served from the brand center. Customer-facing perception studies showed no disruption — exactly what you want from an evolution.

The rollout taught me that brand work at this scale is 20% design and 80% operations, governance, and communication.
